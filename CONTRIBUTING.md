# Contributing

Corrections and evidence-backed improvements are welcome. These repositories are research products, so changes must preserve traceability and comparability.

## Useful contributions

- correcting a factual or transcription error;
- reporting a broken or superseded official source URL;
- adding a newer official publication without overwriting the historical vintage;
- improving documentation, definitions or reproducibility;
- identifying a methodological break, universe change or unit mismatch;
- proposing a new source that is primary, official or technically authoritative.

## Evidence required

A substantive data change should identify, where applicable:

1. the original publisher or institution;
2. the exact public source URL or persistent identifier;
3. publication date and statistical reference period;
4. geographic and statistical universe;
5. unit and precision;
6. whether the value is observed, derived, estimated or a proxy;
7. the rows or fields affected;
8. any consequence for historical comparability.

Screenshots, secondary summaries or search snippets are not sufficient when the underlying source is available.

## Integrity rules

- Do not interpolate or extrapolate missing values unless a specific project methodology explicitly authorizes it.
- Do not replace historical observations with newer vintages without preserving the original series.
- Do not silently change units, territorial definitions or statistical universes.
- Keep operational, planned, announced and derived infrastructure categories separate.
- Preserve source-specific licensing and attribution requirements.
- Treat methodological changes as explicit breaks when continuity cannot be demonstrated.

## Corrections versus new versions

A transcription or metadata correction can be applied to the current working branch and recorded in the changelog.

A change that materially alters published results, methodology or coverage should normally be associated with a new versioned release rather than silently rewriting a citable snapshot.

## How to propose a change

Open a GitHub issue or pull request with a concise explanation, the supporting source and the exact field or file affected. For methodological changes, explain why the proposed treatment is more defensible than the current one.

The project author retains editorial responsibility for the public release and may request additional evidence before incorporating a change.
